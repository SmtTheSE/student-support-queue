A practical answer to a difficult question: when teachers have limited time, which students should they contact first?

Student Support Queue turns exam and study records into a teacher-reviewed priority list. Built from 100,000 records, evaluated against a simple administrative baseline, and connected to a real support workflow.

01 / The decision

An exam result arrives
too late to be an early warning.

Students who struggle need support while there is still time to act. Teachers need a manageable worklist, especially when tutoring, check-ins, and staff time are limited.

This project frames prediction around that constraint. The question is how many students with later scores below 50 can be reached within a fixed number of support slots. That cutoff matches this file’s pass/fail labels.

The product supports a teacher’s judgment. Students can still be referred for help outside the queue.

03 / From prediction to action

A worklist someone can use.

The model gives a priority.
The teacher and student agree on the support.

01

Freeze the snapshot

Use previous achievement, attendance, assignments, and verified study measurements recorded before the exam.

02

Set available slots

Rank the cohort by estimated failure risk. Capacity comes from staff time, not a default probability threshold.

03

Review the need

Check learning gaps, incomplete assignments, and attendance barriers. Offer a printed or in-person route when needed.

04

Follow through

Record contact, participation, completion, exam outcomes, and cost. Learn whether support makes a difference.

A small, practical output

The scoring tool accepts an unlabeled pre-exam CSV and a slot count. It validates the records and creates a ranked teacher worklist with recorded support needs, access considerations, and missing-input counts.

05 / Evidence you can inspect

Built to make the claim traceable.

Read the executed notebook ↗

Leakage prevention

Current-exam scores, grades, answer counts, and outcome labels never enter scoring. Ambiguous timing fields are excluded. Pre-exam availability remains a condition for use.

Separate evaluation

60% training, 10% calibration, 10% selection, and 20% testing. Baseline comparisons use identical slot counts. Bootstrap intervals show sampling uncertainty.

Group-level checks

Gender and socioeconomic fields are excluded from ranking. Errors and selection rates are audited by group; excluding a field alone does not establish fairness.

Where the evidence stops

The source and collection times are unverified. There are no school IDs, subjects, exam dates, or intervention outcomes. A random holdout measures this file’s patterns; it does not establish results in future exams or new schools. A prospective local pilot is the next decision.
